Thaw 3.0 for macOS 27 is in beta, with [a new design](/roadmap/thawui), and Thaw 2.1 for macOS 26 is out. Thaw 2.2, also in beta, brings back macOS 15. Thaw 2 gets no more new features, only bug fixes, performance work and security updates. Thaw is getting smaller: the larger features are becoming modules you add only if you want them. That plan came from the [discussion in #1353](https://github.com/thaw-app/Thaw/issues/1353). This is the plan for the next year as it stands, not a promise, and your reports decide the order.

<Roadmap>

<RoadmapList title="Now" count={3} state="now" here note="What is being built, for the next releases of Thaw 3 and Thaw 2.">
<RoadmapGroup name="macOS 27" count={2}>

- **Thaw 3.0.0.** In beta. It is a rebuilt menu bar core for macOS 27. The latest beta is 3.0.0-beta.4 and the latest alpha is 3.0.0-alpha.12.
- **Modules.** Scripts, triggers and automation become optional downloads, so you install only the parts of Thaw you use. Since beta.4 the sidebar gathers Profiles, Automation and Experiments under a Modules heading, ahead of the move.

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="macOS 15 and 26" count={1}>

- **Thaw 2.2.0.** In beta. Since 2.2.0-beta.1, Thaw 2 runs on macOS 15 Sequoia again. From here the Thaw 2 line gets only bug fixes, performance work and security updates.

</RoadmapGroup>
</RoadmapList>

<RoadmapList title="Up next" count={5} state="next" note="These follow Modules.">
<RoadmapGroup name="Layout" count={1}>

- **Undo for drags.** Command-Z already undoes keyboard moves, and drags within a section in the experimental editor window. Layout’s own drags come next.

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="From 2.1, as modules" count={4}>

- **Scripts.** Scripts that make their own menu bar items, tested by macOS 26 users on 2.1.0, come to 3.0 as a module you can add.
- **The full trigger engine.** In 2.1 an item can move on battery level, the frontmost app, a network, a Focus and more. 3.0 has part of it: 3.0.0-beta.2 added showing items while an app runs. The rest comes as a module.
- **Widgets.** One is in 3.0 so far: a custom status icon with three slots for meters, added in 3.0.0-alpha.3.
- **Rotating diagnostic logs.** Keeping and cycling through diagnostic logs will appear in a later 3.0 build.

</RoadmapGroup>
</RoadmapList>

<RoadmapList title="Blocked by macOS 27" count={3} state="blocked" note="These run into limits in macOS 27 itself. The team is looking for ways around them and can’t promise them for the beta.">
<RoadmapGroup name="Hiding" count={1}>

- **Separate items from the same app.** macOS 27 hides and shows an app’s items together, so one of an app’s items can’t yet go in Hidden while another stays in Visible.

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="Layout" count={2}>

- **Even spacing.** Each app adds its own padding around its items, so the same spacing setting can still leave uneven gaps.
- **A Timer stand-in.** Thaw can’t remove Apple’s Timer, so it can’t put one of its own in its place.

</RoadmapGroup>
</RoadmapList>

<RoadmapList title="Further out" count={9} state="later" note="These come after the move to modules. The team intends to build them, and none has a date.">
<RoadmapGroup name="Integrations" count={5}>

- **An Alfred workflow.** One you can install, on top of the `thaw://` links that [already work with Alfred](/docs/thaw/uri-schemes).
- **Import from another menu bar manager.** Bring your setup with you, so switching to Thaw doesn’t mean starting over.
- **More with Droppy and Floe.** Both already drive Thaw through `thaw://` links: they open menu bar items and apply profiles. The plan is to take that further. <Marks of="droppy floe" />
- **OmniWM.** Work alongside the OmniWM window manager on macOS 26 and 27, so Thaw stays out of the way of its workspaces. It came up on [OmniWM’s issue tracker](https://github.com/OmniNull/OmniWM/issues/752). <Marks of="omniwm" />
- **MCP support.** A server for AI assistants, so one you already use can read your menu bar and rearrange it when you ask.

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="Installing" count={1}>

- **Nix and dotfiles.** A flake, so Thaw can be installed and pinned from a Nix configuration, and a page listing where Thaw keeps its settings, so they can live in a dotfiles repo with nix-darwin, chezmoi or similar. <Issue n={1363} /> <Marks of="nix" />

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="Across your Macs" count={1}>

- **iCloud backups.** For profiles, appearance and preferences, as a few options in the panes they belong to, not a new pane. Anything tied to one Mac’s displays stays local. <Marks of="icloud" />

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="Menu bar items" count={2}>

- **Your own menus in the menu bar.** Make an item yourself and fill its menu with actions: open a link, run a Shortcut, run a script. Thaw puts it in the menu bar and treats it like any other item.
- **Isolation mode.** Show just one item for a while, with everything else out of the way. <Issue n={970} />

</RoadmapGroup>
</RoadmapList>

<RoadmapList title="Ideas" count={3} state="later" note="Things we have written up and not decided on. None is planned.">
<RoadmapGroup name="Integrations" count={2}>

- **Stream Deck and other controllers.** Keys that hide and show sections, switch profiles and stay lit while a mode is on.
- **Items hosted by Thaw.** Another app hands Thaw an icon and a menu, and Thaw shows it as a menu bar item that follows your sections and profiles like any other.

</RoadmapGroup>
<RoadmapGroup name="Displays" count={1}>

- **OLED saving mode.** The menu bar sits on the same pixels all day. Thaw could turn it true black, shift the items a point or two now and then, and dim them when the Mac is idle.

</RoadmapGroup>
</RoadmapList>
